3ASD_STYLE_INSTRUCTION = ( 

4 "Use ASD-STE100 principles as a strong guide, with roughly 80 percent " 

5 "adherence across the prose. Do not claim formal compliance. Prefer " 

6 "clear, simple words, active constructions, and one main idea per sentence. " 

7 "Keep terms consistent and technical names exact. Vary sentence length " 

8 "so the prose has a natural rhythm and does not sound like a repeated " 

9 "template. Assume the reader can read code but does not know this project. " 

10 "Use familiar words in place of avoidable jargon. When a technical term " 

11 "is needed, explain it in plain English before using it; expand acronyms " 

12 "on first use. Introduce the basic concept before its implementation name. " 

13 "Do not replace precise terms with vague substitutes. Make only claims " 

14 "supported by the evidence. Apply these rules to every prose field, " 

15 "including titles, summaries, the preamble, and closing." 

16)

18WHOLE_STORY_INSTRUCTION = ( 

19 "Edit and organize the complete walkthrough as one document. Keep useful " 

20 "mechanism detail, direct citations, and a short lead separate from the " 

21 "required project/basic-concept preamble. Explain concepts before listing " 

22 "class or field names. Before using `base` or `head`, define them as the " 

23 "earlier and later compared revisions. Explain that a snapshot is the " 

24 "captured comparison input with file evidence from those revisions before " 

25 "using terms such as snapshot validation or input envelope; prefer captured " 

26 "input to envelope. When `blob ID` or Git blob terminology matters, explain " 

27 "that it names a stored file-content object linked to the supplied file " 

28 "text. Explain only concepts needed for this change; do not turn the " 

29 "preamble into a glossary. Define necessary jargon such as NUL and UTF-8 " 

30 "at first use. Group formatting and mechanical changes compactly; " 

31 "explain a mechanism once when a demo and source repeat it. Remove writer " 

32 "part caveats when the complete document contains the implementation, and " 

33 "connect each mechanism to its explanation. Keep source warnings honest. " 

34 "Near the opening, make the quick summary a paired concrete case, not a " 

35 "mechanism paraphrase. Name one input and its evidence conditions, state " 

36 "the specific result before the change, then use the same case to state " 

37 "the specific result after it. Use a case shown by the supplied source; do " 

38 "not invent input details or outcomes. If no concrete case is established, " 

39 "label that plainly. Add a short route to mechanism, tests, and " 

40 "compatibility passages. State an installation or compatibility " 

41 "consequence only when supplied evidence supports it. In test passages, " 

42 "say what the test asserts or checks. Keep overall execution status in the " 

43 "shared verification display and relevant attribution. Do not repeat a " 

44 "generic not-run or not-verified caveat in each test passage; retain " 

45 "source-specific uncertainty. Use zero analyzer-verified runs; never invent a model field " 

46 "for that host-owned value. Quote author-reported checks only as exact " 

47 "substrings of the supplied description. End with specific remaining " 

48 "review questions linked to passages and relevant static test IDs. Never " 

49 "claim execution, equivalence, or behavior that source evidence does not " 

50 "establish. For a report with about 150 changes and 24 records, aim for " 

51 "roughly 3,000 narrative words as guidance, not a universal limit. Scale " 

52 "the length to the evidence and keep the reading path useful." 

53)

55PREAMBLE_INSTRUCTION = ( 

56 "Write a required preamble for the 'Before the code' section. First " 

57 "introduce the project or relevant part of the system: what it does, " 

58 "who or what uses it, and the problem it solves, as far as the supplied " 

59 "evidence establishes. Name the project when the evidence identifies it. " 

60 "Early in the preamble, give one or two concrete motivating examples, " 

61 "not only in the separate quick summary. For each example, describe a " 

62 "specific task or input, the problem or result before the change, and " 

63 "what the change enables for the same case. Show why the difference " 

64 "matters to the user or system. Explain the basic concepts through these " 

65 "examples before describing the general mechanism and reading path. " 

66 "Ground examples in supplied source or tests, or in writer orientations " 

67 "and passages when editing. A constructed example must follow behavior " 

68 "established by that evidence and be clearly labeled as illustrative, " 

69 "not a reported incident. If the earlier behavior or an outcome is not " 

70 "established, say so instead of inventing it. Do not assume prior knowledge " 

71 "of the project. Do not invent project background when it is not known. " 

72 "Keep this separate from the short introductory sentence and from the " 

73 "implementation tour. Do not name real files, paths, functions, identifiers, " 

74 "commands, or source lines in the preamble. This restriction concerns " 

75 "implementation names, not the known project name. Use conceptual role labels. " 

76 "Use about 200 to 450 words for a large change when the evidence supports " 

77 "that length; use less for a small change and do not pad. Separate " 

78 "paragraphs with a blank line. Allow at most 4,000 characters of prose " 

79 "and a separate 4,000 characters for complete fenced sketches, including " 

80 "their fences; keep the total within 8,000 characters. Include one small " 

81 "conceptual sketch when it helps explain the system or decision, using " 

82 "a fenced plain-text block marked 'text'. Do not use Mermaid. A two-path " 

83 "decision sketch has exactly three lines: a short label, then " 

84 "'├─ condition → outcome' and '└─ condition → outcome'. Other sketches " 

85 "remain text. Distinguish author-reported goals from source evidence." 

86)
